Data Collection for Physical AI

How to collect data interesting for physical AI? High-fidelity data requires synchronized multi-view cameras, low-latency teleoperation, and a diverse set of initial conditions to ensure the policy generalizes.

What makes data useful? Useful data captures not just the successful completion of a task, but the subtle corrective movements made by the human operator. It should cover edge cases and varied environments.

Evaluation Benchmark: We demonstrate that starting with our dataset is highly useful for the community. We provide benchmarks on overall success rates and success rates per motion (e.g., picking is highly reliable, while task-specific tool use requires more precision).

Failure

Understanding failures is crucial for advancing physical AI. We analyzed several modes of failure in our policies:

  • Bad grasping
  • Unexpected pausing
  • Misplacement of objects
  • Collisions
  • Dropped objects

Oopsie-Data: Sometimes, suboptimal success or failure provides valuable learning signals. We draw inspiration from Oopsie, noting that failure states can be used to train more robust recovery policies.

Future Work (DAgger): We plan to incorporate DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) using history (actions + images) to iteratively correct these failure modes during teleoperation.

Method

Hardware Setup & Teleoperation

We utilize open-sourced hardware (Gello) and software (Lerobot) to enable intuitive teleoperation for data collection. The setup includes multiple camera viewpoints to capture the intricate details of chemical manipulation.

Diffusion Policy for Chemists

How it works: Instead of programming exact movements, we teach the robot by showing it examples. The "Diffusion Policy" takes in what the robot sees (camera images) and its current position (input), and gradually refines a plan of action until it produces the correct next movement (output).

Think of it like a chemist learning a new titration technique: you observe the setup, remember your training, and continuously adjust your hand movements based on the color change in the flask.
